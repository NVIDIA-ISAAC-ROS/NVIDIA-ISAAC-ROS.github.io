==============================================================
Improve Real-World Performance with SPARR (Experimental)
==============================================================

.. note::

   SPARR is an experimental research project and is not part of the supported Isaac for
   Manipulation workflow.

SPARR (Simulation-based Policies with Asymmetric Real-world Residuals) keeps the
simulation-trained DisplayPort insertion policy as a base policy and learns a small residual
policy on the real robot. The residual policy observes wrist camera images and the robot state,
learns from a sparse success reward, and adds a correction to the base policy's action, so
real-world training only has to close the sim-to-real gap. Use it to boost success rates on top
of the simulation base policy, including harder settings with socket pose observation noise.

The `SPARR repository <https://github.com/NVlabs/SPARR>`__
implements this for DisplayPort insertion on a Flexiv Rizon 4s with two RealSense D405 wrist
cameras. Its README covers installation, demonstration collection, residual training, and
evaluation.

Results
-------

The residual policy was trained for about 50 minutes on the robot, using about 150 to 200
real-world demonstrations that were collected fully automatically. The following table compares
the success rates of the base policy and SPARR under socket pose noise:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1

   * - Socket pose noise
     - Base policy success rate
     - SPARR success rate
   * - 1 mm
     - 93.3%
     - 100%
   * - 2 mm
     - 63.3%
     - 91.1%
   * - 3 mm
     - 33.3%
     - 77.7%

SPARR also handles some offset in the robot's starting pose, such as when the plug starts above
the socket but not perfectly aligned with it.

For details on the method, refer to the `SPARR paper <https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23253>`__.
